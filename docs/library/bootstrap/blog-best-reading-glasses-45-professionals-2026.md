## The #1 Mistake: Ignoring Your PD (Pupil Distance)

Here's what most online eyewear stores won't tell you: the most important number for your glasses isn't your prescription. **It's your PD — the distance between your pupils, measured in millimeters.**

Get this wrong by 2mm, and your brain compensates all day. By 3pm, you have a headache. By 5pm, you're rubbing your eyes. For progressive lenses (the ones that let you see near AND far without switching glasses), an inaccurate PD is the #1 reason people say "I can't adapt to progressives."

Most online stores give you two options:

- Use a ruler against your screen (good luck getting that accurate)
- Enter a "standard" PD (which doesn't exist — every face is different)

**For professionals 45+, precise PD matters more.** You're likely going from perfect distance vision to needing near correction. Your brain is adapting to a new visual reality. The last thing it needs is a 2mm error on top of that.

## How to Choose Frames That Don't Make You Look Old

The frame is where most people go wrong. They pick what's "safe" — and safe usually means 10 years older than they are.

**Three rules for choosing frames at 45+:**

- **Fit is everything.** The frame width should match your face width. Too narrow = accentuates sagging. Too wide = looks like you borrowed someone's glasses. Check the frame width measurement (in mm) on every product page — it should be within 5mm of your face width.

- **Brow line matters.** After 45, the mid-face starts to soften. Frames with a strong brow line (the upper rim of the frame) draw attention upward. This is the same principle behind a good haircut — direct the eye to where you want it.

- **Material signals age.** Full-rim acetate frames read as "contemporary." Thin metal frames read as "traditional." For the same lens, an acetate frame will make you look 5 years younger than a metal frame. This isn't opinion — it's visual psychology.

**The goal isn't to look 25.** It's to look sharp, present, and engaged. Your glasses should say "I take care of myself" — not "I gave up."

## Progressive Lenses: What They Don't Tell You

If you're 45+ and need reading glasses, you've probably heard of progressive lenses. They let you see near, far, and everywhere in between — without switching between two pairs of glasses.

What most online stores don't tell you: **not all progressives are equal.**

There are two tiers:

- **Standard progressives** (most online stores' default): Harder to adapt to. The "swim effect" — where things look wavy at the edges — lasts longer. Some people never fully adapt.

- **Premium progressives** (Essilor, ZEISS): Designed with wider clear zones. Adaptation is faster. Visual quality is noticeably better.

The difference isn't small. It's the difference between "I hate my progressives" and "I forgot I'm wearing them."
