## Why Your PD Matters More Than Your Prescription

Your pupillary distance (PD) is the distance between the centers of your pupils, measured in millimeters. It's the single most important number for comfortable glasses — more important than your prescription itself.

Get your PD wrong by 2mm and your brain compensates all day. By 3pm you have a headache. By 5pm you're rubbing your eyes. For progressive lenses (the ones that let you see near and far without switching glasses), an inaccurate PD is the **number one reason people fail to adapt.**

Most online eyewear stores ask you to enter your PD but give you no reliable way to measure it. They suggest holding a ruler against your screen or entering a "standard" PD. There's no such thing as a standard PD — it ranges from 54mm to 78mm in adults, and every face is different.

Here are three methods to measure your PD at home, ranked from least to most accurate.

## Method 1: The Ruler (Least Accurate)

**What you need:** A millimeter ruler and a friend.

**How to do it:**

- Sit 20 inches (50cm) from your partner
- Place the ruler against your eyebrows
- Have your partner align the zero mark with the center of your left pupil
- Read the measurement at the center of your right pupil
- Repeat 3 times and take the average

**Why it's inaccurate:** The ruler bends on your face. Your partner's angle is never perfectly straight. Human eyes make micro-movements every few seconds. Most people get a measurement within 3-4mm of their true PD — which is enough to cause headaches with progressive lenses.

**Accuracy range:** ±3-4mm

**Verdict:** Better than guessing. Not good enough for progressives.

## Method 2: The Printable Card (Marginally Better)

**What you need:** A standard credit card (or a printable PD card from an eyewear website) and a ruler.

**How to do it:**

- Hold the card against your forehead, aligned with your pupils
- Mark where each pupil center falls on the card
- Measure the distance between marks with a ruler

**Why it's slightly better than a bare ruler:** The card is rigid, so it doesn't bend. But you're still relying on a partner to mark your pupils, and the card doesn't sit at the same distance from your eyes as glasses do.

**Accuracy range:** ±2-3mm

**Verdict:** A small improvement over the ruler method. Still not precise enough for progressive lenses.

## Method 3: Phone Camera with Computer Vision (Most Accurate)

**What you need:** Your smartphone (front camera) and 30 seconds.

**How to do it:**

- Open the PD measurement tool on your phone's browser
- Position your face in the frame (like a selfie, but hold the phone at arm's length)
- Look straight ahead at the camera
- The tool uses computer vision to detect your pupils and calculate the distance between them
- Result appears on screen in millimeters

**Why it's more accurate:** Computer vision algorithms (like MediaPipe, the same technology used in medical imaging applications) detect your pupils with sub-millimeter precision. There's no ruler bending, no partner angle error, and no guessing. The measurement is based on the actual pixel distance between your pupils, calibrated to your camera's known focal length.

**Accuracy range:** ±0.5-1mm

**Verdict:** The most accurate at-home method available. No tools, no partner, no guessing.

## What Happens If Your PD Is Wrong

If you're buying single-vision reading glasses (just for near work), a 2mm error is uncomfortable but tolerable. Your brain adapts, you get mild headaches, and you might blame the glasses for being "cheap."

If you're buying **progressive lenses**, a 2mm error is catastrophic. Progressive lenses have a narrow "corridor" of clear vision in the center. If your PD is off, you're looking through the wrong part of the lens. The result:

- Everything looks slightly warped at the edges (the "swim effect")
- Your eyes work harder to find the clear zone
- After a few hours, you get headaches, eye strain, or nausea
- Most people give up and say "I can't wear progressives" — when the real problem was the PD measurement

**This is why precise PD measurement matters more than anything else when buying glasses online.** Get this right and everything else falls into place. Get it wrong and even the best lenses in the world will feel uncomfortable.

## How to Verify Your PD Is Correct

After measuring, here's a quick sanity check:

- **Normal adult PD range:** 54mm to 78mm
- **Average for women:** ~62mm
- **Average for men:** ~64mm
- **If your measurement is below 54 or above 78:** Remeasure — you likely made an error

If you have your current glasses, you can also check the PD by looking at your prescription from your eye doctor. It should be listed as a single number (distance PD) or two numbers separated by a slash (each eye's PD from center of nose).

A ruler measurement might work for cheap readers, but it's the difference between adapting in 3 days and never adapting at all.
