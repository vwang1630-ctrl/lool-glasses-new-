Every luxury gorilla sofa on the market today falls between $899 and $32,000. That is a 35x price range for what looks like the same thing: a gorilla-shaped couch. The difference is not the shape — it is what is inside, what has been tested, and what happens when something goes wrong.

Here is exactly what a $9,900 gorilla sofa buys that a $899 one does not.

---

## The Frame: Steel vs Guesswork

A gorilla sofa is not a rectangle with cushions. It has shoulders, a chest, arms that curve under, and a back that leans. Every curve is a structural stress point.

The [Kong Tender Titan](https://fuzzsofa.com/en/products/kong-tender-titan-monumental-sculpture-sofa.html) is built on a fully welded Q235 steel skeleton. The foam is 45kg/m³ cold-cure — it does not shift, sag, or compress unevenly over time. The entire frame is engineered to distribute weight evenly across every curve.

This matters when a 90 kg adult sits on the gorilla's lap. Or when three people sit on it at a party. Or when a child jumps on the shoulder. The frame does not care — it was designed for this.

## The Test: 200 kg Dynamic, 300 kg Static

We load-tested the Kong to 200 kg for daily use and 300 kg for static load, with a 5.5× safety factor over the rated capacity. That means the frame can handle more than five times what you will ever put on it.

Every serialised inspection report (e.g. SH2024-0945) travels with the crate. You can read the exact numbers for your specific piece — not a generic claim, but the recorded result of the test on your sofa.

## The Fur: Long-Pile, Hand-Applied

The fur is long-pile faux — not a print, not a texture pattern, but individual pile fibres that catch light the way real fur does. It is applied by hand over the foam sculpting, which means the direction of the pile follows the muscle lines of the gorilla form.
